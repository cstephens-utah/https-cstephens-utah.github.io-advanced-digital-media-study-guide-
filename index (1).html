Course at a glance

Digital Media Advanced is the second-year course in the digital media pathway. Students plan, design, develop, evaluate, revise, and publish digital and interactive projects using text, 2D and 3D graphics, animation, sound, video, and digital imaging. The Utah standards identify grades 10–12, prerequisite Digital Media 2, and Skill Certification Test #815.

Strand 1 — Advanced skills, visual design, and copyright

  • Apply advanced techniques to 2D graphics, 2D animation, digital video, and digital audio.
  • Use visual design elements, color theory, and image composition in interactive projects.
  • Follow the school's Acceptable Use Policy (AUP), understand Fair Use, and comply with copyright law.
Key idea: Professional media is technically polished, visually intentional, audience-focused, and legally sourced.

Strand 2 — 3D graphics and animation

  • Create a 3D wireframe model and modify vertices, edges, and faces.
  • Add surface materials and textures; set cameras and key, fill, and back lights.
  • Render a model.
  • Animate with keyframes or along a path on a Cartesian coordinate system.
Workflow: Model → materials → camera/lighting → animate → render and review.

Strand 3 — Interactive media project workflow

  1. Pre-plan: define concept, goals, objectives, audience, requirements, technology, costs, timeline, team roles, content, and approvals.
  2. Design: prepare a proposal, storyboard, flowchart, script, asset list, navigation plan, interface plan, naming conventions, and directory structure.
  3. Develop: prototype, create and organize assets, assemble the project, add scripting/programming, document the work, and monitor time and costs.
  4. Test and revise: conduct quality assurance (QA), fix issues, gather feedback, revise, and obtain client approval.
  5. Package and publish: prepare distribution materials and user documentation, set a launch date, publish, and plan maintenance.

Strand 4 — Team dynamics and careers

  • Identify interests, technical talents, problem-solving, organizational and leadership skills, innovation, and creativity.
  • Collaborate and understand roles such as graphic artist, designer, programmer, digital media designer/developer, subject matter expert, media/instructional designer, and web specialist.
  • Explore education, training, career pathways, industry trends, and role requirements.

Strand 5 — Interactive digital media portfolio

  • Plan a digital portfolio showcasing work from digital media courses.
  • Show the process—not only final products—including planning, design, creation, and publishing.
  • Build menu-driven navigation and include project designs/storyboards, animation, audio, 2D and 3D graphics, video, finished individual/team projects, and a resume.
  • Follow copyright and Fair Use requirements throughout.

Strand 6 — Work-based learning and competition

Optional experiences may include a business field trip, job shadow, internship, guest speaker, independent/industry work, school/district/state contest, Digital Media Arts Festival, or CTSO competition.
